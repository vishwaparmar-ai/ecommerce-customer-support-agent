# Delivery Exceptions

This document covers what happens when a shipment does not follow its normal path to
delivery.

## Delayed Shipments

A shipment is marked **Delayed** when it has passed its estimated delivery date without
being delivered. Common causes include:

- Courier network congestion or regional disruptions
- Incorrect or incomplete address requiring re-attempt
- Weather or logistical disruptions
- High order volume during peak periods

**What to tell a customer with a delayed shipment:** the order has not been lost, it is
still in the carrier's network, and a new estimated delivery window will be
communicated once available. If a delayed shipment eventually arrives, it proceeds to
Delivered as normal and the standard return window begins from that actual delivery
date, not the original estimate.

## Lost Shipments

A shipment is marked **Lost** when the carrier is unable to confirm its location for an
extended period (typically beyond 15 days past the estimated delivery date) and
declares it undeliverable. This is distinct from Delayed — a lost shipment will not
arrive.

**Resolution for lost shipments:** these are handled as a support case, not a standard
return, since there is no item to physically return. Support staff should verify the
shipment status with the carrier, then offer the customer either a replacement order or
a full refund of the original payment.

## Failed Delivery Attempts

If a delivery agent is unable to deliver a package (customer unavailable, incorrect
address, refused delivery), the shipment typically returns to **In Transit** or
**Out for Delivery** for a re-attempt rather than being marked Delayed immediately,
unless the estimated delivery date has already passed.

## Escalation

Any delivery exception that a customer has already contacted support about more than
once, or that involves a high-value order, should be escalated to a human agent rather
than resolved automatically.
