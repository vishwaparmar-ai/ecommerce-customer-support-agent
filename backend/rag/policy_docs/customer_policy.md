# Customer Service Standard Operating Procedure

## Ticket Priority Levels

Support tickets are assigned one of four priority levels:

- **Low** — general questions, minor cosmetic issues, non-urgent requests.
- **Medium** — standard order/return/refund issues affecting one customer.
- **High** — payment discrepancies, repeated failed deliveries, time-sensitive
  complaints.
- **Critical** — potential fraud, account security concerns, safety-related product
  issues, or significant financial impact.

## Ticket Status Lifecycle

1. **Open** — ticket has been created and not yet picked up.
2. **In Progress** — an agent is actively working the ticket.
3. **Waiting Customer** — the agent needs more information from the customer before
   proceeding.
4. **Resolved** — the issue has been addressed; awaiting confirmation or auto-close.
5. **Closed** — the ticket is fully closed.

A resolved or closed ticket can be reopened back to In Progress if the customer
responds with a related follow-up.

## When to Escalate to a Human Agent

The following situations should always be escalated rather than resolved
automatically:

- Low confidence or conflicting information about the customer's issue.
- Any suspicion of fraud or account security compromise.
- High-value orders or unusual compensation requests.
- Ambiguous policy situations not clearly covered by existing policy documents.
- Repeated failed resolution attempts on the same issue.
- The customer explicitly asks to speak to a human.
- Any action that failed partway through and left an inconsistent state (e.g. a refund
  that started processing but did not confirm completion).

## Response Expectations

- Acknowledge every ticket promptly.
- Reference the customer's actual order/return/refund data rather than general
  statements when specific records exist.
- Do not make promises about outcomes (replacement, refund amount, timelines) that
  are not backed by an actual policy or a completed action.

## Auditability

Every ticket creation, status change, and assignment is logged with who made the
change and when, so any ticket's history can be reconstructed for review or dispute
resolution.
